The Problem
01 / Motivation
Digitized does not mean understood

The Annals are searchable.
They are not yet askable.

Searchable archive

Find a known word

→
Askable archive

Begin with a research question

The system helps discover records whose historical vocabulary you did not yet know.

03Expert-Grounded Validation
14 / Two Judgments
Retrieval quality has two different meanings

A high similarity score is not yet
historical relevance.

Machine judgment

How close are the vectors?

Automatic metrics can test whether the retriever learned a defined correspondence.

“Did the system retrieve what it was trained to retrieve?”
Historical judgment

Can this record serve as evidence?

A historian decides whether the returned source actually bears on the research question.

“Would I use this record to construct an answer?”
03Expert-Grounded Validation
15 / Human-in-the-loop
Beyond automated scores

Quality must include the judgment of
the researchers who will use the RAG.

Quantitative evaluation

Measure what the system retrieves.

Automatic scores show whether retrieval changes.

→
Expert blind evaluation

Ask whether the evidence is useful.

Domain researchers judge the results without knowing which model produced them.

→
Return to design

Let that judgment reshape the RAG.

The users' expertise becomes part of quality control.

This is human in the loop: researchers do not merely use the system; their judgment helps define its quality.
